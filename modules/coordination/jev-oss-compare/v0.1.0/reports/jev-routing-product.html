The expensive part is not the typo

Agents fail in three ways that cost you an afternoon, and every one of them was visible before the tool ran.

You said: “Use the hosted voice API. Keep the key in the env file. Do not self-host.”

The plan said: clone the upstream repo, pip install -e, start a local server.

Nothing crashed. Nothing looked wrong in the diff. The agent simply reinterpreted your constraint and charged ahead — and you found out at the end of the session, not at the moment of the turn.

Constraints, skimmed

A long instruction list becomes a suggestion list. The one line that mattered gets acknowledged, then quietly ignored.

“Impossible”, unverified

“That needs a paid plan” or “there is no way to do this locally” ends the session — sometimes correctly, sometimes without anyone checking.

Unsafe moves, allowed

A destructive command or a key written to the wrong file is the same kind of miss: the request contradicted something you already decided.

What Jev routing is

A set of small decision gates that sit between “the agent wants to act” and “the agent acts.” Each gate reads your pinned constraints and the request about to happen, then answers one question: is this still the thing you asked for?

Pin what you decided

Hosted-only. No secrets in the repo. Ship behind a flag. Each constraint is recorded once, in plain words, and stays on file.

Gate the next move

Before a risky tool call, a plan boundary, or a strong claim, a cheap Jev check compares the moment against your pins. A few hundred milliseconds, not another full model pass.

Route the recovery

A “no” is never bare. The gate recommends a route: ask you again, redraw the plan, look something up, verify with a second agent, or stop and escalate.

Six routes, one plain idea

Every gate decision lands on one of these. The agent keeps moving; you keep the wheel.

reconfirm_intent

Ask before acting

The request brushes against a constraint the agent may have skimmed. It repeats your words back and checks the reading before it moves.

rethink_plan

Redraw the plan

The next step no longer matches why you started. The gate sends the plan back for a revision instead of letting the drift ship.

research_more

Look it up first

A confident claim arrived with no evidence behind it. The route asks for a quick search pass before the claim steers any work.

dispatch_verifier

Prove it separately

The work looks done, so a second, independent checker verifies the result against your criteria before anyone celebrates.

escalate

Bring the human

Sometimes the call is genuinely yours. The gate stops routing guesses, shows you the conflict, and asks.

proceed

Carry on

Constraints match, claims are grounded, the move is safe. The most common route is the boring one: nothing to see, keep working.

Where this stands

Honesty is part of the product. Here is what is real today, and what is not claimed.

  • The routing architecture runs as coordination modules in Agent Custom Setup, with adapters for Claude, Codex, and Kilo workflows.
  • The Jev decision lane is live: the current run scored 69 of 69 gate decisions on one session stream, median 238 ms, for about $0.002 total. The numbers are on the technical page.
  • Not measured yet: accuracy, catch-rate, or recovered task outcomes. The runs so far are plumbing checks, not a benchmark verdict.
